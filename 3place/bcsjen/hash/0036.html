0003 apron

Pronunciation

/ˈeɪ.prən/ (AY-pruhn)

Etymology & Root

  • Middle English apron (uncertain origin, possibly from Old French naperon “small tablecloth”).

English-to-English explanation

A protective garment worn in front of the body, often with a strap or bib, such as a cook’s apron or work apron.

Example:

He wore a huge leather apron that came right up to his left shoulder, and bulging round his middle with a hammer, a red handkerchief, a powder-horn, all kinds of objects tucked into the belt used as a pocket.

English-to-English explanation

A skilled craftsman or worker in a trade, such as a smith, weaver, or baker.

Example:

He was a tall man, half peasant-farmer, half artisan.

0006 bosom

Pronunciation

/ˈbʊz.əm/ (BUZ-uhm)

Etymology & Root

English-to-English explanation

Example:

Meanwhile, at the words the peasant had spoken – ‘Would you be the man?’ – the woman had risen, picked up both children in her arms and rushed to take refuge behind her husband, staring at the stranger in terror, her bosom uncovered and with fear in her eyes, murmuring quietly, ’Tso-maraude.
