0001 baron

Pronunciation

/ˈber.ən/ (BAIR-ən)

Etymology & Root

  • Old French baron “man, warrior”.

English-to-English explanation

A nobleman of the lowest rank; a powerful businessman.

Example:

In the dining room, a long and splendid gallery on the ground floor looking out over the gardens, Monseigneur Henri Puget had on the twenty-ninth of July 1714 held a ceremonial banquet for Monseigneurs Charles Brûlart de Genlis, prince-archbishop of Embrun; Antoine de Mesgrigny of the Capuchin Order, bishop of Grasse; Philippe de Vendôme, grand prior of France, abbot of St-Honoré de Lérins; François de Berton de Crillon, baron-bishop of Vence; César de Sabran de Forcalquier, seigneur-bishop of Glandève; and Jean Soanen, Oratorian priest, chaplain in ordinary to the king, seigneur-bishop of Senez.

0002 cause

Pronunciation

/kɔːz/ (kawz)

Etymology & Root

  • Latin causa “reason, cause”.

English-to-English explanation

A reason or justification; a principle or movement; the plaintiff in a lawsuit.

Example:

It could not be said which gave greater cause for wonder, his pallor or his serenity.

English-to-English explanation

To make someone believe something false.

  • Middle French destingué “distinguished” (from Latin distinguere “to mark off, separate”).

English-to-English explanation

Marked by excellence; respected; eminent.

0005 episcopacy

Pronunciation

/ɪˈpɪskəpəsi/ (ih-PIHS-kuh-pə-see)

Etymology & Root

  • Late Latin episcopatus “episcopate” (from episcopus “bishop”).

English-to-English explanation

The office, rank, or system of bishops; the government of the church by bishops.

Example:

Some time after Monsieur Myriel was raised to the episcopacy the emperor had made him a baron of the Empire, at the same time as several other bishops.

0006 frail

Pronunciation

/freɪl/ (frayl)

Etymology & Root

  • Middle English freyl “weak” (from Old French fraile “fragile, delicate”).

English-to-English explanation

Weak in body or health; delicate; morally weak.

Example:

Even his attitude towards Napoleon had been accepted and tacitly pardoned, so to speak, by the people, his flock of frail good-hearted souls who adored their emperor but loved their bishop.

0007 hatred

Pronunciation

/ˈheɪtrɪd/ (HAY-trid)

Etymology & Root

  • Middle English hatered “hatred” (from hate + -red).

English-to-English explanation

Intense and persistent dislike; animosity.

Example:

I want to destroy human inevitability; I condemn slavery, I chase out poverty, I instruct ignorance, I treat illness, I light up the night, I hate hatred.

0008 outline

Pronunciation

/ˈaʊtlɑɪn/ (OUT-layn)

Etymology & Root

  • Old English ūtline “outline” (from ūt “out” + line).

English-to-English explanation

A line or sketch showing the shape or main features; to give a brief description.

Example:

Although Monseigneur Bienvenu could not have been less of a politician, this is perhaps the place to outline very briefly what his position was with regard to the events of that time, assuming that Monseigneur Bienvenu ever thought of having a position.

0009 pastoral

Pronunciation

/ˈpæs.tɚ.əl/ (PAS-tər-əl)

Etymology & Root

  • Latin pastor “shepherd”.

English-to-English explanation

Relating to the countryside; concerning a priest’s duty to parishioners.

Example:

Some time afterwards the general council, taking his request into consideration, voted him an annual sum of three thousand francs that was registered as ‘Allowance awarded to the bishop for carriage expenses, mail-coach expenses, and the expense of pastoral visits’.

0010 patriot

Pronunciation

/ˈpeɪ.tri.ət/ (PAY-tree-ət)

Etymology & Root

  • Greek patris “fatherland”.

English-to-English explanation

A person who loves and defends their country.

Example:

We would be in great danger of deceiving ourselves if we were to conclude from this that Monseigneur Bienvenu was ‘a philosopher bishop’ or ‘a patriot parish priest’.

English-to-English explanation

A place set aside for a specific purpose (e.g., a national park); a place where a group (especially Native Americans) is kept; a stipulation or limitation.

0012 rural

Pronunciation

/ˈrʊr.əl/ (ROOR-əl)

Etymology & Root

  • Latin ruralis “of the countryside”.

English-to-English explanation

Relating to the countryside rather than towns.

Example:

The June Days signalled the failure of Republicanism, whose support was limited to an urban minority in a largely rural society.

0013 summon

Pronunciation

/ˈsʌmən/ (SUM-uhn)

Etymology & Root

  • Old French sommoner “to summon” (from Latin summoneo “to remind, warn”).

English-to-English explanation

To order or request someone’s presence; to call for a meeting.

Example:

传唤；召集（summon 的过去分词）

0014 synod

Pronunciation

/ˈsɪnəd/ (SIN-ud)

Etymology & Root

  • Greek synodos “assembly” (from syn “together” + odos “way”).

English-to-English explanation

A meeting of church officials for discussion or decision-making; a governing body of churches.

Example:

Monsieur Myriel was summoned by Napoleon to the synod of bishops of France and Italy, convened in Paris thereafter.

0015 tacitly

Pronunciation

/ˈtæsɪtli/ (TAS-uht-lee)

Etymology & Root

  • Latin taciturnus “silent” (from tacere “to be silent”) + adverb suffix.

English-to-English explanation

In a tacit manner; without being spoken; implied or understood without being expressed.

Example:

Even his attitude towards Napoleon had been accepted and tacitly pardoned, so to speak, by the people, his flock of frail good-hearted souls who adored their emperor but loved their bishop.
