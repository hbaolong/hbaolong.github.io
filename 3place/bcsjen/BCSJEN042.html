This detail is significant and one we think worth while expanding on – we would almost say, emphasizing.

From time immemorial Montreuil-sur-Mer’s specialized industry had been the imitation of English jet jewellery and of the black glass jewellery of Germany. This industry had always languished because of the high price of the raw materials, which affected the workers’ pay. When Fantine returned to Montreuil-sur-Mer an amazing transformation had taken place in this production of ‘black ware’. Towards the end of 1815 a man, a stranger, had come and set up business in the town, and had the idea of substituting shellac for resin in the manufacturing process and, for bracelets in particular, using metal clasp links that were simply pinched together instead of soldered. This very small change had been a revolution.
